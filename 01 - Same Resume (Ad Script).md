# Campaign 01 — "SAME RESUME"

**Format:** Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts / pre-roll · 9:16 (16:9 safe) · **55 seconds**
**Language:** Telugu–English mix · burned-in captions (most viewers watch on mute)

---

## 1. Concept
Placement season, seen from the **recruiter's side of the table**. Hundreds of students, one resume template: *Python. Java. MS Office. Quick learner. Team player.* Nobody is lying, but nobody stands out, because everyone learned from the same scattered playlists and none of them built anything.

The ad's uncomfortable truth: **listing a skill isn't the same as proving it.** KloudCourse is the bridge: structured learning, then practice, then something you can actually show.

**Title:** SAME RESUME
**Closing line:** అందరిలా list చేయకు. Build చేసి చూపించు.

## 2. Primary objective
Consideration + sign-up intent. Reframe the problem from "I need more courses" to "I need learning that makes me *able to show* something". That is the gap structured modules and practical assignments fill.

## 3. Target audience
Final-year students and freshers (20–24) heading into or stuck in placement season. They have certificates and a resume, but no confidence when asked "what have you built?"

**Psychological trigger:** fear of being interchangeable, followed by the relief of a clear path. It's not fear of unemployment, which is overused and preachy.

---

## 4. Opening hooks (genuinely different angles)

| # | Hook | Angle |
|---|------|-------|
| 1 | Tired recruiter flips through resumes: *"Python. Java. MS Office. Quick learner…"* (flip) *"ఈ రోజు ఇది 37వ resume. అన్నీ same."* | Perspective flip: the student sees themselves from the other side |
| 2 | Close-up on a student reading their own resume, then a mirror reveal: 20 students behind them holding the identical page. *"నీ resume లో ఉన్న ప్రతి skill… నీ పక్కనోడి దాంట్లో కూడా ఉంది."* | Visual shock / identity |
| 3 | Black screen, white text: *"Python తో ఏం build చేశావ్?"* Then a ticking timer and 3 seconds of awkward silence. | Discomfort / silence as a hook |
| 4 | *"47 hours of tutorials watched. ఒక్క working app కూడా లేదు."* YouTube history scrolls endlessly. | Tutorial-hell confession |
| 5 | A phone shows 63 open tabs. *"ఇవన్నీ 'later చూస్తా' అని open చేసినవి."* | Relatable humour |

### Recommended: Hook 1
- **Perspective nobody shows students.** Every EdTech ad talks *to* the student. This one lets them overhear what a recruiter actually thinks. That's curiosity-driven, not lecture-driven.
- **Works on mute.** Identical resumes flipping is instantly readable without sound.
- **Self-recognition in about 2 seconds.** Every final-year student has "Quick learner, Team player" on their resume, so the laugh-then-wince lands fast.
- Hook 3 (the silence) is too strong to waste. It becomes the **mid-story turning point** instead.
- Hooks 4 and 5 are good for follow-up cutdowns or a series (see the end of this file).

## 9. Music & sound design
- **0–13 s:** no score. The rhythm comes from paper flips and a drone. Silence makes the hook feel real, not like an ad.
- **13–21 s:** sound vacuum. Only the clock. This is the emotional low point.
- **21–27 s:** a muffled, uneasy synth pad and city noise.
- **27 s onward:** a modern, warm electronic pulse (think lo-fi beat turning into an uplifting build, not an EDM drop). Each ✓ in the montage hits a beat.
- **End:** a short, ownable logo sting. Reuse it in every campaign to build sonic identity.

## 10. Brand reveal & CTA
- The brand appears **only at 0:49**, after the problem is proven and the transformation is shown.
- The reveal grows from the opening image (the resume pile), so it reads as the story's ending, not an ad break.
- **CTA:** "Start learning today", with the link or QR. No pricing or discount (none provided).

---

## Creative director notes
- **Claims check:** we never promise a job. The callback ends on the interviewer *leaning in*, not an offer letter. "37వ resume" is a character's line, not a statistic. Every feature shown (modules, quizzes, flashcards, assignments, the listed course areas) is from the brief.
- **Casting:** Ravi should look ordinary, not a model. The recruiter's dry humour carries the hook, so cast a strong comic-timing actor.
- **Possible weak spot:** the "turn" at 0:27 depends on VFX quality. If the budget is tight, replace the tab corridor with a practical effect: a desk buried in sticky notes that Ravi sweeps into one neat column.
- **Series potential:** Hook 4 ("47 hours of tutorials") and Hook 5 ("63 tabs") can each become 15-second cutdowns using the same visual world. That gives you a 3-ad series for retargeting.
